|---|---|---|---|---|

**What this means.** The ratings gap is large: League Two's starters sit about 19 points below the Premier League's on every passing and first-touch attribute (`20260930-ratings-gap-eng1-eng4.md`). Four rating responses in the engine are wrong:

1. **Passing hardly responds to skill.** Accuracy moves 0.5 points over 24 rating points, and real squads even show the gradient reversed. The causes are the physics shortcuts:
   - the receiver always knows the ball's true path;
   - heavy touches come straight back: 85% are gathered again by the receiver at every quality;
   - execution error depends weakly on skill.
   - **Fixes:** 2.3b and 2.3e.
2. **Attack grows with quality faster than defence.** From q58 to q82, goals go from 3.1 to 4.5 and shots from 28.5 to 41.2. That's ENG1's shot excess (0.60 a minute against 0.41–0.49), and part of ENG4's goal deficit.
   - Real goals and shots are similar across leagues, so the creation and finishing mechanics must scale with defending quality too.
   - **New finding.** Its cause still has to be traced (shot selection, pressure on the shooter, blocks, keepers). It's queued as a rating-balance question after 2.3e (see `progress.md`).
3. **Fouls grow with absolute ratings.** They go from 4.9 to 16.8: duel volume comes from absolute attributes, not situations. **Fix:** 2.4 (duels from situations).
4. **Better sides play more long balls.** The share goes from 12% to 17%, the reverse of real football.
   - With real squads it's worse: ENG4 plays only 3.6% long balls and ENG1 9.8% (Opta PL 11.7%; League Two more).
   - Honest estimates (2.3c) should make weaker passers prefer safer, longer options.
   - The real-squad gap to synthetic sides (3.6% against 12–13% at similar quality) points at club instructions or roles, to be checked in 2.3c/2.3e. A style chosen from the squad's strengths belongs in C2, never a league rule.

## 3. Passers' estimates aren't honest

Estimate minus completion, averaged per band:

| | Short | Medium | Long | Cross |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ENG4 | −0.095 | −0.102 | +0.007 | +0.168 |
| ENG1 | −0.059 | −0.064 | +0.099 | +0.230 |

- Short and medium passes are underrated by 6–10 points.
- Crosses are overrated by 17–23 points, and ENG1's long balls by 10.
- So the choice is biased towards crosses and long balls from good positions, and away from simple passes.
- **Fix:** 2.3c, with the reliability table as the acceptance check.

## 4. Passes are too slow

Travel time of completed passes, against Metrica's open data (`20260930-pass-pace-metrica.md`):

| | Short | Medium | Long |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engine, ENG4 / ENG1 (s) | 1.33 / 1.27 | 2.15 / 2.14 | 3.16 / 3.13 |
| Metrica (s) | 0.99 | 1.60 | 2.76 |

- Engine passes are about 30% slower over short and medium distances, and about 14% over long ones.
- Slow passes give defenders time to close lanes. That's part of why `intercept_scale` had to fall to 0.2.
- **Fix:** 2.3b, pass pace from the physics (`ROLL_FRICTION` and arrival pace moved to YAML), then `intercept_scale` rechecked.

## 5. Where failed passes go

ENG4, per match:
- loose 58.8: a stopped or misplaced ball, or a heavy touch, collected by the opponent;
- intercepted 20.5;
- out for a throw-in 14.8, for a goal kick 5.4;
- lost in the air 4.3;
- offside 7.0.

ENG1 has fewer loose balls (44.8) and more throw-ins (25.4).
