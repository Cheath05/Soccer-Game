# Step 2.3b part 1: what the reception fix did (30 Sep 2026)

**The change** (fa82a79):
- the receiver reads the pass after a delay set by his anticipation, heading first for where it was meant to go;
- a heavy touch locks its player out for 0.6 s;
- a cross that lands clear follows the ground-pass completion rule.

**Measured** with f020e2 settings, seed 21 and 200 matches per batch, against the 2.3a reference (`20260930-step2.3a-summary.md`). The reports are local, under `reports/engine/step2.3/ref-2.3b1/` and `sweep-2.3b1-q58|q82/`.

## The side effect: the build-up leaks again

| | ENG4 2.3a | ENG4 2.3b1 | ENG1 2.3a | ENG1 2.3b1 | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High regains | 29.1 | 47.4 ±0.8 | 23.5 | 35.8 ±0.8 | 10–17 |
| Loose failures | 58.8 | 112.4 | 44.8 | 77.9 | |
| Heavy touches | 71.7 | 64.9 | 52.8 | 46.3 | to be sourced |
| Goals | 2.28 | 2.82 | 3.50 | 3.72 | PL 2.65–3.05; EFL 2.45–2.85 |
| Shots | 27.3 | 30.1 | 37.0 | 39.1 | PL 23–27.5; EFL 22–26 |

**Why.**
- Misplaced and miscontrolled balls now really are lost: heavy touches lost to the opponent went from about 15% to about 50%.
- Too many of those balls come in the passing side's own half, and the opponent wins them high up.
- Heavy touches run at 46–65 a match, which looks high for professional football. I couldn't verify FBref's per-match "miscontrols" figure, so there is no target yet: a reference to source, not a number to tune to.

**Where it gets fixed, in plan order (no league constant, no widened range):**
- **2.3b part 2 (pace):** faster passes change how long opponents have to reach a misplaced ball.
- **2.3c (honest estimates):** the passer sees the real risk of a pass, including the receiver's first touch under pressure, so pressed defenders choose safer options.
- **2.3e (rating terms):** `control.receiver` and `control.touch_skill`, so first touch decides heavy touches and their frequency comes from ratings; plus the read-delay range.

**The acceptance check** is still 2.3e's: high regains no worse than f020e2's (ENG4 29, ENG1 24), and towards 10–17.
