# Step 2.1: home advantage fitted to real football (16 Oct 2026)

**Result:** `home_advantage.yaml` crowd decisions and execution 0.3 → **0.85**, referee card
0.15 → **0.08**, referee foul 0.05 → **0.02**; the fast engine's `home_advantage` 1.15 →
**1.10**. One set of values for every league. Code measured: 8db15b2 (the fit) and 189b5ac (the
share gaps and pass completion), from the measure worktree, values from config copies; the
batch reports are under `reports/engine/2.1/` (not committed). **One conflict is recorded**
(below): with the crowd carrying all of home advantage, the away side's extra fouls and its
shortfall in pass completion run above real football's.

## Method

- **Mirrored batches** (`calibrate-engine --mirror`, 3655ed3): each pair drawn plays twice,
  home and away, as every pair does in a league season. With random pairs, which side happens
  to be at home moves the home figures by more than the targets' width: two plain 200-match
  ENG1 samples of one engine had given home .305 / away .455 and .36 / .335. Intervals
  resample whole pairs.
- **400 matches (200 pairs) a batch**, ENG1 and ENG4, real squads, no focus club, seeds 31-33.
- **Each mechanism fitted to its own trace** in real football (football-data E0-E3, 2023/24 to
  2025/26, every match: 1,140 PL, 1,656 a division in the EFL; `match_targets.yaml`):
  - the crowd to the home goal difference, read through the home xG difference (half as
    noisy; goals follow it pooled, +0.23 against +0.21 over the batches, though a single batch
    can differ by ±0.14), with the home side's extra shots as a check;
  - the referee's card value to the away side's extra yellows;
  - the referee's foul value to the away side's extra fouls.

## Reading

- **The engine has no lean of its own.** With every value at 0: goal diff -0.015 ±0.159, xG
  diff +0.022 ±0.069, shot gap +0.17 ±0.62.
- **The old crowd value was about a third of what real home advantage needs** (+0.10 xG at
  0.3). The response bends upwards (+0.35 at 1.0). **0.85 lands it:** pooled over seeds 31-32
  (800 pairs) home xG diff **+0.258 ±0.034**, goal diff **+0.323 ±0.082**, home - away wins
  **+0.110** (real +0.11 to +0.14), shot gap **+2.68 ±0.25**, shots on target **+0.78**. Seed 33
  (final values, 400 pairs): xG +0.216 ±0.050, goals +0.195 ±0.104.
- **The crowd carries the whole of home advantage.** Behind closed doors in 2020-21 home
  advantage shrank but didn't vanish (McCarrick et al. 2021, 15 leagues), so familiarity and
  travel play a part too. The engine models neither, so their share sits in the crowd values.
  0.85 is inside the mechanism's range (at most 1), and match totals hold: pass accuracy .885 /
  .884 / .884 at 0 / 0.3 / 1.0, fouls, interceptions, high regains and ball in play unchanged.
  Goals rise a little with the crowd (2.94 → 3.15 → 3.27 at 0 / 0.3 / 1.0 on the same
  fixtures): a gift to the home side is worth slightly more than the same cost to the away
  side.
- **Referee.** Card 0.15 gave a card gap of +0.42 pooled at crowd 0.75 (real about +0.31): part
  of the gap is behaviour (the away side fouls more under the crowd). 0.08 gives +0.34 pooled
  (seeds 31-32) and +0.25 (seed 33), 7.3% and 8.4% of the yellows (real 6.7-9.3%). The foul
  value: the batches can't tell 0.05 from 0.02 apart (+0.95 pooled with 0.05, +0.94 with 0.02,
  on different fixtures, so the difference carries about ±0.36; the mechanism alone predicts
  about 0.25 less). The reason it's small is arithmetic: 0.05 on its own is a 5% gap in fouls,
  more than real football's whole gap (2.2% in the PL, 3.8% in League Two), and the engine's
  away side already fouls more from behaviour.
- **The fast engine** (it plays every match the user doesn't watch) had a stronger edge than
  real: `cross-engine` on the seed-33 fixtures, 20 replays each, gave +0.376 (ENG1) and +0.390
  (ENG4) at 1.15. At 1.10: +0.259 and +0.272, home / draw / away .434 / .239 / .328 and
  .428 / .260 / .312, real football's split.

## A conflict: the crowd carries all of home advantage

Real home advantage comes partly from the crowd and partly from familiarity, territory and
travel (it shrank but didn't vanish behind closed doors). The engine models only the crowd and
the referee, so to reach real football's home edge in results the crowd values carry it all,
and the effect is concentrated where a player is under pressure: with a man right on him an
away player's pass error and decision noise are 1.43 times neutral and a home player's 0.58
times (at pressure 0.5, 1.21 and 0.79). On that comparison an away passer of 85 under
moderate pressure is as error-prone as a home one of about 49 (the reviewer agent's
arithmetic), and the home/away gap for one player is larger than composure's whole range.

What shows: results, the home side's share of the shots and the card gap match real football;
the away side's extra fouls run 1.5-2.4 times real; the home side's pass completion gap (1.0-1.6
points) is near the indicative real +0.7 in points, but large next to the engine's pass error
(11.6% in ENG1, 9.3% in ENG4, against real 15-26%).

**Kept, and recorded rather than hidden:** cutting the crowd until fouls and passing look right
would leave home advantage at about half its size in results, which every league table shows.
The fix is a second mechanism for the non-crowd part (familiarity with the ground, territory),
so the crowd values can come down: a follow-up, and a design decision for the user.
